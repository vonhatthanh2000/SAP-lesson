Choose the aggregate boundary

Before writing CDS syntax, decide what changes together, what has an independent lifecycle, and which entity owns transactional consistency.

The tangible win

Design the root and child topology for the Sales Order Change & Release Workbench, then defend composition versus association using lifecycle—not database cardinality.

This lesson builds on your Lesson 1 result: the business object owns the invariant, so its boundary must include the state needed to enforce that invariant.

Start with the business transaction

A sales coordinator creates one request, adds items, submits it, and an authorized user releases or holds it. The request is incomplete without its items; a customer or product exists independently of this request.

Root: SalesOrderRequest

Owns identity, organizational context, sold-to reference, requested date, currency, total, status, and audit fields.

Child: Item

Owned by one request. Holds product reference, quantity/unit, simplified unit price, derived net amount, and requested date.

References: Customer and Product

Exist independently and are referenced by association. Deleting a request must not delete customer or product master data.

Composition is lifecycle ownership

Question Composition Association
Can the target exist meaningfully without this parent? Usually no Usually yes
Who owns creation/deletion lifecycle? The aggregate root The referenced entity or another BO
Where do lock and authorization commonly originate? Root; child may depend on it Each independent BO owns its concerns
Is cardinality alone enough to decide? No No

Dissect the OpenSAP aggregate first

In the sample, Travel is the root. It owns a composition of Bookings but only associates to Agency, Customer, and Currency. The Booking child points to its parent and independently references Customer, Carrier, Connection, Flight, and Currency.

Boundary drill

Decide before opening the explanation.

Order Item: composition or association?

Composition. The item is created and deleted through its request and participates in request-wide invariants such as “a submitted request has at least one valid item.”

Sold-to Customer: composition or association?

Association. The customer exists before and after this request. The request references customer identity; it does not own customer lifecycle.

Product: composition or association?

Association. Product master data is independently managed. An item references it and may snapshot selected commercial values where the simplified model requires them.

Should Item be a second RAP root because it has its own table?

No. A separate persistence table does not imply an independent business-object lifecycle. Root choice follows transactional ownership, not table count.
