Lesson 11 · 60–75 minutes · SAP SD foundation

Trace one sale from customer request to accounting

Learn the document chain before learning configuration. For each order-to-cash step, identify the responsible role, the document it creates, and the business state it changes.

Why this is your first deep SD lesson

You already understand a RAP root/child aggregate, transactional state, validations, and actions. Standard SD adds a different idea: the complete sale is usually represented by a flow of related business documents, not by one giant mutable object. That is the bridge between your RAP knowledge and functional SD reasoning.

Tangible win: given a customer order, explain in 90 seconds why Sales Order, Outbound Delivery, Goods Issue, Billing Document, and Accounting Document are different records—and name the operational or financial effect of each.

The business story

Bike World asks your company for 10 bicycles. The company confirms the sale, warehouse staff ship the goods, billing invoices the customer, and Finance records and clears the receivable. Ask one question throughout the lesson:

What is now true in the business that was not true before this step?

The backbone: document, owner, effect

Inquiry→Quotation→ Sales Order→Outbound Delivery →Goods Issue→ Billing→Payment

Inquiry and quotation are optional presales documents. A common stock-sales path begins with the sales order, continues through an outbound delivery and goods issue, and then reaches billing and Finance. SAP’s current learning material presents the same core sequence and uses process flow to navigate the relationships between documents: Navigating the Order-to-Cash Process Steps.

Do not confuse document creation with its decisive posting

Delivery created

The warehouse has an execution document. Picking and packing can be planned or recorded.

Goods issue posted

The physical issue becomes an inventory event, with quantity and value consequences.

Billing created

The receivable and revenue side can be posted to Financial Accounting.

This distinction is a frequent source of weak explanations. “The delivery reduces stock” is too vague: the decisive event in the standard flow is posting goods issue. Likewise, billing and payment are separate: billing establishes what the customer owes; payment settles it. See SAP Learning’s explanation of the Sales Order–Receivables integration.

Look inside the Sales Order: three levels

LevelScopeExamplesQuestion it answers
HeaderWhole sales documentSold-to party, sales area, overall datesWho is this transaction with, and under which commercial context?
ItemOne product/service lineMaterial, ordered quantity, item category, conditionsWhat is being sold, and how is this line processed?
Schedule lineQuantity/date commitment within an itemConfirmed quantity, requested/confirmed dateHow much can be delivered, and when?

An item can have more than one schedule line when quantities are confirmed for different dates. This is why your RAP portfolio’s Request → Item model is a useful simplification, but not yet a full representation of standard sales-order execution. SAP’s official lesson describes the same header, item, and schedule-line structure: sales document structure.

Document flow is lineage, not one status field

The order-to-cash process can split. An order for 10 bicycles might produce a delivery and invoice for 6 now, with 4 remaining open for later fulfillment. The linked documents and their quantities explain what happened more precisely than a single header status.

RecordQuantityMeaning
Sales-order item10The customer ordered 10.
First delivery / PGI6Six have physically left.
First invoice6Six have been billed in this example.
Open requirement4Four remain to be fulfilled, subject to configuration and process status.

RAP transfer: Lesson 10 modeled explicit status transitions inside one custom Request BO. Standard SD also needs status, but document flow records lineage across separate business documents. Do not model a real order, warehouse execution, billing, and FI posting as one custom RAP aggregate merely because they form one business story.

In ABAP Cloud or SAP S/4HANA Cloud, use released CDS views, business objects, events, and APIs available for the target edition. SAP publishes current Sales interfaces in APIs for Sales.

Failure-tracing sequence

  1. Start with the business symptom and identify the last document that exists.
  2. Open that document and inspect its status, blocks, quantities, and document flow.
  3. Decide whether the missing result is a document creation, goods movement, accounting posting, or clearing event.
  4. Inspect the responsible document level and source configuration; do not begin with a random table.
  5. For an interface failure, compare the API response and application log with the actual committed document state.

Your 15-minute tracing exercise

Without looking at the table above, draw seven boxes from customer interest to payment. Under every box write:

  1. the business document or posting,
  2. the role that owns the step,
  3. one fact that becomes true, and
  4. one tempting but incorrect effect that has not happened yet.

Then explain these two failure scenarios:

  1. A delivery exists, but PGI was not posted. What work can exist, and what inventory event is still missing?
  2. A billing document exists, but no payment was received. What does the customer owe, and which team owns the next step?

Retrieval questions — answer one at a time

  1. What is the difference between order-to-cash and a single Sales Order?
  2. Which steps are optional before the Sales Order?
  3. What does a Sales Order record, and what physical event has not happened?
  4. Why are Outbound Delivery and PGI separate?
  5. Which event changes inventory quantity and value?
  6. How do billing and incoming payment differ?
  7. What belongs at header, item, and schedule-line level?
  8. Why can one order item lead to multiple downstream documents?
  9. How is SD document flow different from a RAP composition?
  10. For a partial delivery of 6 from an ordered 10, what remains open?
