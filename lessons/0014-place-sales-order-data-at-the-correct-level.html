Place Sales Order data at the correct level

Learn to decide whether a business fact belongs to the Sales Order header, an item, or a schedule line—and trace how header defaults, item exceptions, quantities, dates, and confirmations interact.

Prerequisite gate

Begin after you can distinguish reusable master data from the transaction-specific values copied into a Sales Order. Review the Lesson 13 recap if you cannot yet trace payment terms or partner data back to their sources.

Tangible win: inspect any Sales Order field and defend its level using this question: what is the smallest business scope for which this value remains true?

The whole model

Sales Order header          one commercial transaction
  ├── Item 10               one product/service line
  │     ├── Schedule line 1 one quantity/date commitment
  │     └── Schedule line 2 another quantity/date commitment
  └── Item 20
        └── Schedule line 1

SAP describes sales documents as a header with multiple items. Each item can contain one or more schedule lines. Header data is relevant to the whole document, item data is specific to one product or service line, and schedule-line data divides an item's requirement by quantity and date. See SAP Learning's Executing Sales Order Management and SAP Help's Item Schedule Line.

The hierarchy is about business scope, not screen position. A field shown near the top of a UI is not necessarily a header field, and a repeated display value may still have been copied into each item.

Level 1: header — true for the order as a whole

Header data establishes the common commercial context. Put a fact at header level when it is intended to apply to the complete Sales Order rather than one particular product or delivery commitment.

Typical header factWhy it belongs here
Sales Order number and typeIdentify and control the complete document.
Sales AreaProvides the commercial context for the order.
Sold-to PartyOwns the overall commercial relationship.
Customer purchase-order referenceUsually identifies the customer's order as a whole.
Document currencyProvides the common currency context for document values.
Overall blocks and statusSummarize or control the entire document.

“Usually” matters. SAP supports item-level exceptions for some data, including partners, payment-related values, blocks, texts, and conditions. The correct level follows business meaning and configured behavior, not a universal list.

Level 2: item — true for one product or service line

An item answers what is being sold and how that line is processed. Two items in the same order may use different Materials, Plants, quantities, pricing, delivery rules, or rejection reasons.

Typical item factWhy it belongs here
Material or serviceIdentifies the subject of this line.
Ordered quantity and sales unitDescribe how much of this line was requested.
Delivering PlantA different source location may fulfill each line.
Item net valueRepresents the commercial result for this line.
Item categoryControls how this particular line participates in the process.
Item rejection reasonOne line may be rejected while others continue.

The item does not merely repeat header information. It is its own processing unit inside the document. The header can summarize the items, but a total or overall status does not erase their individual values and states.

Level 3: schedule line — one quantity at one date

A schedule line divides the demand of one item according to quantity and date. It can hold requested and confirmed delivery information, delivered and open quantities, and delivery-relevant controls. SAP defines this node as the division of an item by date and quantity in the Sales Order API documentation.

Typical schedule-line factBusiness meaning
Requested delivery dateWhen the customer wants this quantity.
Schedule-line order quantityHow much demand belongs to this line.
Confirmed delivery dateWhen SAP currently confirms fulfillment.
Confirmed quantityHow much SAP currently confirms for that date.
Delivered/open confirmed quantityHow much has progressed and how much remains due.
Schedule-line delivery blockWhether this particular commitment may proceed.

Schedule lines are not extra product lines. They are time-and-quantity subdivisions of the same item.

Requested is not the same as confirmed

Customer requests 10→ ATP checks availability→ 6 confirmed earlier+ 4 confirmed later

Suppose BikeWorld orders 10 bicycles for 10 September. Availability allows only six by that date, with four more available on 17 September. The business meaning is:

LevelQuantityDateMeaning
Item10—Total ordered quantity for the bicycle line.
Schedule line A6 confirmed10 SepFirst fulfillment commitment.
Schedule line B4 confirmed17 SepRemaining fulfillment commitment.

The exact schedule-line representation depends on the availability result and configuration, but the invariant is the important part: the item expresses overall demand; schedule lines express dated fulfillment commitments. SAP explains that ATP results are recorded in schedule lines and can allow partial deliveries in Running an ATP Check.

A downstream delivery is based on due, delivery-relevant confirmed schedule-line quantities—not merely on the item's original ordered quantity.

Header defaults and item exceptions

Some header values are copied to items so all lines begin with a consistent commercial context. That does not mean the item has no value of its own. 

Initial state
Header payment terms: 30 days
Item 10 payment terms: 30 days  ← aligned with header
Item 20 payment terms: 45 days  ← explicit exception

Change header to 60 days
Item 10: follows the header change
Item 20: may retain its different item value

SAP Help explains that when header and item values are identical, later header changes can be copied to those items; an item whose value differs represents an exception. Follow-up processing can add further restrictions. See Relationship Between Header and Items.

Do not say “the item inherits dynamically.” A more useful model is that SAP copies or propagates selected values under field-specific rules. An item exception can stop ordinary propagation for that value.

Values also roll upward

Information does not travel only downward. Item values and states also contribute to header totals and overall status. 

Schedule-line confirmations → item confirmation/delivery state
Item values                 → header/document net value
Item statuses               → overall document status

An overall status is therefore a summary, not an independent claim. If Item 10 is complete and Item 20 is open, the header may be partly processed. To diagnose the cause, move down the hierarchy instead of trusting only the summary.

Technical-consultant lens: keys, tables, and API nodes

Use VA01, VA02, and VA03 to create, change, and display Sales Orders in classic SAP GUI; VA05 is a common list transaction. In a Fiori or API scenario, the UI entry point changes, but the header/item/schedule business hierarchy remains.

Business levelClassic key and persistence landmarkReleased/API-style node
HeaderVBAK-VBELNA_SalesOrder
ItemVBAP-VBELN + POSNRA_SalesOrderItem
Schedule lineVBEP-VBELN + POSNR + ETENRA_SalesOrderScheduleLine

These table names are useful landmarks for classic-system debugging, but they do not define the business semantics by themselves. In ABAP Cloud or clean-core extensions, use released CDS views, business objects, and APIs available in the target system rather than assuming direct table access is an appropriate integration contract.

Why the keys matter

VBELN                 identifies the Sales Order
VBELN + POSNR         identifies one item inside that order
VBELN + POSNR + ETENR identifies one dated quantity line inside that item

This composite-key shape encodes containment in classic persistence. It also explains why querying VBEP by order number alone mixes schedule lines from every item, and why aggregating quantities without the correct grain creates duplicate or inflated results.

Debug at the correct grain

  1. Reproduce the issue in display mode and note order, item, and schedule-line identifiers.
  2. Use F1 technical information when available to identify the field and data element.
  3. Determine whether the value is stored, copied, derived, or aggregated before choosing a table or API node.
  4. Read header, item, and schedule data using their full keys; compare requested, confirmed, delivered, and open quantities.
  5. Check follow-up documents before treating a value as freely changeable.

Do not use SE16N/SE16H as your first model of the business object. They can help authorized consultants inspect persistence in applicable systems, but released APIs/CDS carry semantic and lifecycle contracts that raw tables do not.

Transfer the structure to your RAP portfolio

SalesOrderRequest                         " root / header
  ├── composition [0..*] → Items         " product lines
  │     └── composition [0..*] → Schedules " dated quantities
  └── composition [0..*] → OrderPartners

Possible ownership
  Request:  SalesArea, SoldTo, Currency, OverallStatus, TotalAmount
  Item:     Product, OrderedQuantity, Unit, Plant, UnitPrice, NetAmount
  Schedule: RequestedDate, ConfirmedDate, RequestedQty, ConfirmedQty

A schedule is lifecycle-dependent on its item, and the item is lifecycle-dependent on the request, so composition expresses the ownership chain. Product, Business Partner, Plant, and Sales Area remain independent associations.

Keep the portfolio honest: your custom `Schedules` entity can model a simplified promise plan, but do not claim that it reproduces standard SAP ATP, scheduling, requirements transfer, or delivery processing. Those are integration boundaries for later work.

Your 30-minute structure exercise

  1. Draw one order containing two items; give Item 10 two schedule lines and Item 20 one.
  2. Place these fields: Sold-to, Material, Sales Area, Plant, Document Currency, Ordered Quantity, Confirmed Quantity, Requested Date, Confirmed Date, Item Net Amount, Total Amount, and Rejection Reason.
  3. For every field, say why moving it one level upward would lose meaning or moving it downward would duplicate a common fact.
  4. Model an order for 10 units with six confirmed on the requested date and four confirmed later.
  5. Change a header default after Item 20 has a deliberate exception. Predict which item follows the header and defend why.
  6. Draw the equivalent RAP composition tree and mark every independent master object as an association.
